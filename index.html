The Student visa (subclass 500) allows international students to study full-time at a CRICOS-registered Australian institution. It covers higher education, vocational training, English language courses, and schools. Holders must maintain enrolment and satisfactory course progress as conditions of visa grant. The visa can be extended if studies continue at a registered provider, and family members can be included as secondary applicants.

  • Valid Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from CRICOS-registered provider
  • Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) requirement satisfied
  • Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for duration of stay
  • English language proficiency (IELTS 5.5+ overall or equivalent)
  • Evidence of sufficient funds (tuition + AUD $21,041/year living costs)
  • Valid passport with at least 6 months validity
  • Satisfactory health and character checks
  • If under 18: approved welfare and accommodation arrangements

The Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) allows international students who recently graduated from an Australian institution to live, study, and work in Australia after their student visa expires. Two streams exist: Graduate Work (for skills on the skilled occupation list) and Post-Study Work (for bachelor's degree holders or above). This visa provides valuable Australian work experience critical for future skilled migration applications. It is non-renewable, and most applicants use this period to build CRS points or secure a state nomination.

  • Applied within 6 months of course completion notification
  • Australian degree, diploma or trade qualification from a registered provider
  • Studied in Australia for at least 2 years (Post-Study Work stream)
  • Occupation on relevant skilled list (Graduate Work stream)
  • Under 50 years of age at time of application
  • Valid Overseas Visitor Health Cover (OVHC)
  • Competent English: IELTS 6.0 overall (or equivalent)
  • Health and character requirements satisfied

The Skilled Independent visa (subclass 189) is a permanent residence visa for skilled workers not sponsored by an employer or state/territory government. It is points-tested via SkillSelect, requiring an invitation from the Minister based on an Expression of Interest (EOI). Applicants must have a positive skills assessment from the relevant Australian assessing authority and meet the 65-point threshold. This is one of Australia's most sought-after permanent residence pathways, granting full residency rights from day one of grant.

  • Invitation from the Minister via SkillSelect EOI
  • Occupation on the Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL)
  • Positive skills assessment from relevant assessing authority
  • Score 65+ points on the points test (subject to current cut-offs)
  • Under 45 years of age at time of invitation
  • Competent English: IELTS 6.0 each band or equivalent
  • Satisfactory health and character requirements
  • No previous refusal under certain circumstances without disclosure

The Skilled Nominated visa (subclass 190) grants permanent residence to skilled workers nominated by an Australian state or territory government. It follows the same EOI process through SkillSelect as the subclass 189, but state nomination adds 5 points to your score. Each state publishes its own occupation list and requirements, which often differ from the federal MLTSSL. Nomination does not guarantee a visa — applicants must still meet all Department of Home Affairs requirements.

  • Nomination by an Australian state or territory government
  • Occupation on the nominating state's occupation list
  • Invitation via SkillSelect after state/territory nomination accepted
  • Positive skills assessment from relevant assessing authority
  • 65+ points including 5 points for state nomination
  • Under 45 years of age

The Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) visa (subclass 491) is a 5-year provisional visa for skilled workers willing to live and work in a designated regional area of Australia. It requires nomination by a state/territory government or sponsorship by an eligible family member in a regional area. After 3 years of regional living and working, holders may be eligible for the Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) subclass 191. This visa carries 15 points on the points test, making it highly competitive for those in regional-friendly occupations.

  • Nomination by state/territory government or eligible family member sponsorship
  • Occupation on relevant state/territory occupation list
  • Positive skills assessment from relevant assessing authority
  • Score of at least 65 points on the points test
  • Under 45 years of age
  • Commitment to live and work in a designated regional area for 3 years
  • Competent English (IELTS 6.0 each band or equivalent)
  • Health and character requirements met

The Temporary Skill Shortage (TSS) visa (subclass 482) allows Australian employers to sponsor skilled overseas workers to fill genuine skill shortages on a temporary basis. Two streams exist: Short-term (up to 2 years, STSOL occupations) and Medium-term (up to 4 years, MLTSSL occupations). The visa requires employer sponsorship approval, a nomination, and a visa application. It replaced the 457 visa in 2018 with stricter requirements including a mandatory labour market testing obligation. Medium-term stream holders can apply for permanent residence through ENS subclass 186.

  • Employer must be an approved TSS sponsor
  • Nominated occupation on STSOL or MLTSSL as applicable
  • Labour market testing conducted within 4 months prior to nomination
  • At least 2 years relevant work experience in the nominated occupation
  • Minimum salary at or above TSMIT (AUD $73,150 p.a. from 2023)
  • Skills assessment (required for some occupations)
  • English proficiency — Vocational English minimum
  • Health and character checks satisfied

The Employer Nomination Scheme (ENS) visa (subclass 186) is a permanent residence visa for skilled workers nominated by an approved Australian employer. Three streams exist: Direct Entry (newly hired overseas workers), Temporary Residence Transition (TRT — for 482 holders with 3 years employer experience), and Labour Agreement. The ENS is one of the most common employer-sponsored permanent residence pathways in Australia, providing full permanent residency from grant. The nominated occupation must be on the MLTSSL for most streams.

  • Employer holds or obtains standard business sponsorship (SBS) approval
  • Genuine nomination with position on MLTSSL
  • At least 3 years full-time work with sponsoring employer (TRT stream)
  • Under 45 years of age (some exemptions apply)
  • Skills assessment or recognised Australian qualification
  • Competent English: IELTS 6.0 each band or equivalent
  • Salary at or above TSMIT (AUD $73,150+) and market salary rate
  • Health and character requirements met

The Partner visa is a two-stage visa for the spouse or de facto partner of an Australian citizen, permanent resident, or eligible New Zealand citizen. The onshore pathway (subclasses 820/801) allows applicants to remain in Australia during processing. Stage 1 (820) is a temporary visa granted while the permanent stage (801) is assessed — this occurs after 2 years from the original application date. Applicants must demonstrate a genuine and continuing relationship. Same-sex relationships are equally recognised under Australian law.

  • Sponsor must be Australian citizen, PR, or eligible NZ citizen aged 18+
  • Genuine and continuing relationship (married or de facto for 12+ months)
  • Comprehensive evidence: shared finances, household, social recognition, commitment
  • Police clearances from all countries lived in for 12+ months
  • Health examinations for both applicant and sponsor
  • Joint application: both sponsor and applicant provide evidence
  • Character requirements met (both applicant and sponsor)
  • Registration of relationship (if applicable, for de facto claims)

The Visitor visa (subclass 600) allows people to visit Australia temporarily for tourism, business visitor activities, or to visit family and friends. Available in several streams including Tourist, Business Visitor, and Sponsored Family. The visa can be granted for up to 12 months with various stay periods (typically 3 months per entry). It does not permit work or study beyond 3 months. A strong home country tie and genuine temporary intent are essential for approval.

  • Valid passport with at least 6 months validity beyond intended stay
  • Genuine tourist, business visitor, or family visit intent
  • Evidence of sufficient funds to support the stay in Australia
  • Strong ties to home country (employment, family, property, assets)
  • Return or onward travel evidence
  • Health and character requirements met

  • Sponsor information required for Sponsored Family stream

Parent visas allow the parents of Australian citizens, permanent residents, or eligible New Zealand citizens to migrate to Australia permanently. Subclass 103 (Migrant) is a queue-managed non-contributory visa with a very long wait (currently 30+ years). Subclass 143 (Contributory Parent) is significantly faster (5–7 years) but requires a higher visa application charge paid in two instalments. Both visas require the "balance of family" test — at least half of the applicant's children reside in Australia.

  • Child in Australia who is a citizen, PR, or eligible NZ citizen (the sponsor)
  • Balance of family test met (more children in Australia than in any other country)
  • Assurance of support bond paid to Centrelink by Australian sponsor
  • Sponsor must be at least 18 years old and an eligible sponsor
  • Health requirements including chest X-ray examination
  • Police clearances from all countries of residence (12+ months)
  • Second instalment of VAC paid before visa grant (Subclass 143)
  • No outstanding debts to the Australian Commonwealth

The UK Student visa replaced Tier 4 (General) in October 2020 and allows non-UK nationals to study at a Higher Education Provider with a track record of compliance or an accredited institution. Applicants must hold a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) from their institution. The visa covers degree-level study and above, including foundation courses leading to a qualifying degree. IELTS UKVI or an approved equivalent is required at B2 level (CEFR) for degree-level courses.

  • CAS reference number from a licensed UK student sponsor institution
  • English proficiency: B2 level (IELTS UKVI 5.5+ overall or equivalent)
  • Sufficient funds: course fees + £1,334/month (London) or £1,023/month (outside London)
  • Valid passport with appropriate validity
  • Parental or guardian consent if applicant is under 18
  • Tuberculosis test result from approved clinic (for certain nationalities)

The Graduate Route visa allows international students who completed a degree at a UK Higher Education Provider to remain in the UK and work, or look for work, at any skill level after their studies. It is unsponsored — no job offer is required. Bachelor's and master's graduates receive 2 years; PhD graduates receive 3 years. It cannot be extended and can only be obtained once — this period is best used to secure a Skilled Worker sponsorship.

  • Completed a qualifying degree at a licensed UK Higher Education Provider
  • Held a Student visa or Tier 4 visa for the qualifying study period
  • Studying in the UK on a Student visa when applying
  • No serious criminal convictions
  • Valid passport
  • NHS Immigration Health Surcharge paid (£1,035/year)
  • Genuine graduate-level intentions in the UK
  • Applied before Student visa expires

The UK Skilled Worker visa replaced Tier 2 (General) in December 2020 and allows workers with a job offer from a UK-licensed sponsor to live and work in the UK. The occupation must meet the skill threshold (RQF Level 3+) and the salary threshold (£38,700 p.a. from April 2024, or the going rate, whichever is higher). Healthcare workers and shortage occupation roles may qualify at lower thresholds. The visa leads to Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) after 5 years of continuous residence.

  • Job offer from a UK-licensed Skilled Worker sponsor employer
  • Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) issued by the employer
  • Occupation at RQF Level 3 or above on the eligible list
  • Salary meeting threshold: £38,700 p.a. or occupation going rate (2024+)
  • English language at B1 level (IELTS 4.0+ in each skill)
  • Sufficient personal funds: £1,270 (unless employer certified)
  • Health and character checks
  • Tuberculosis test if required for your nationality

The Health and Care Worker visa is a sub-category of the Skilled Worker route for medical professionals working for the NHS, NHS-commissioned services, or registered social care providers. It carries significantly reduced visa fees and IHS surcharge exemptions. The route addresses critical workforce shortages in the UK health and care sector. It is open to doctors, nurses, allied health professionals, and adult social care workers meeting the salary and sponsor requirements.

  • Job offer in an eligible healthcare or social care role
  • Employer must be NHS, NHS-commissioned supplier, or CQC-registered
  • Occupation on the eligible Health and Care Worker list
  • Salary at or above occupational threshold (role-specific, lower than general SW)
  • English language: B1 level (IELTS 4.0+ each skill)

The Global Talent visa is for leaders or potential leaders in academia, research, arts, culture, and digital technology. The visa offers significant flexibility with no tied employer, full work rights, and an accelerated path to settlement in 3–5 years.

The UK Family visa allows eligible family members of British citizens or settled persons to join them in the UK. A Canadian Study Permit is required for international students studying in Canada for programs longer than 6 months. Students must be accepted by a Designated Learning Institution (DLI). Under the Student Direct Stream (SDS), students from select countries can access faster processing of approximately 20 days. The permit includes the right to work off-campus up to 24 hours per week during studies (recent reforms may have changed this — eligibility is subject to current IRCC policy).

  • Proof of sufficient funds: tuition fees + CAD $10,000 first year living expenses
  • Valid passport with at least 6 months validity
  • Proof of strong ties to home country
  • English or French proficiency (IELTS 6.0+ or equivalent for most programs)

The PGWP allows international students who graduated from an eligible Canadian post-secondary institution to work in Canada for up to 3 years after graduation. It is an open work permit — no employer sponsorship required. Length corresponds to program duration (minimum 8 months of study = up to 3 years PGWP). This permit is a key pathway to permanent residence through Express Entry and Provincial Nominee Programs. There is a lifetime limit of one PGWP per person.

  • Graduated from an eligible Designated Learning Institution program of at least 8 months
  • Study permit valid or valid within 180 days when applying
  • Applied within 180 days of receiving final transcript/marks notification
  • Minimum 50% of study completed in Canada (with pandemic exceptions)
  • Full-time student status maintained throughout (with exceptions)
  • Valid passport
  • English or French language proof (recent IRCC requirement)
  • Not previously held a PGWP (lifetime limit: one permit)

The Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) program is managed through Canada's Express Entry system for skilled workers seeking permanent residence. Points are calculated using the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) based on age, education, language, and Canadian experience. FSW is for applicants with foreign or limited Canadian work experience. A minimum 67/100 on the selection grid is required before entering the Express Entry pool. Invitations to Apply (ITAs) are issued in regular draws based on CRS score.

  • Minimum 1 year continuous skilled work experience (NOC TEER 0/1/2/3)
  • Minimum 67/100 points on the FSW selection factors grid
  • Language: IELTS CLB 7 in all four skills (reading, writing, speaking, listening)
  • Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) from approved organisation
  • Proof of settlement funds (varies by family size)
  • Intent to reside outside the Province of Quebec
  • Police clearances and medical examination
  • No inadmissibility issues (criminal or health)

The Canadian Experience Class (CEC) is an Express Entry category for foreign nationals with recent skilled work experience in Canada. Designed for temporary foreign workers and international graduates already in Canada who want to transition to permanent residence. A minimum of 1 year of Canadian skilled work experience within the last 3 years is required. CEC applicants typically achieve higher CRS scores due to Canadian work experience bonus points, making draws more accessible.

  • Minimum 1 year full-time (or equivalent part-time) Canadian skilled work experience in last 3 years
  • Work experience in NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation category
  • Language: CLB 7 (TEER 0/1) or CLB 5 (TEER 2/3) in all four skills
  • Legal status to work in Canada when applying
  • Genuine intent to reside outside the Province of Quebec
  • No requirement for Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) in most cases
  • Valid job offer may boost CRS score (not mandatory)

The Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) allows Canadian provinces and territories to nominate immigrants who meet their specific economic and labour market needs. Each province administers its own streams targeting different worker profiles — including Express Entry-linked streams (which effectively guarantee an ITA via a 600-point CRS boost), and base streams with separate paper-based processes. The H-1B visa allows US employers to temporarily employ foreign workers in specialty occupations requiring a bachelor's degree or equivalent. The Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) replaced multiple work visa types in July 2022 as the cornerstone of New Zealand's reformed immigration system.
